# Creative packets and originality methods

Use this reference when exploration must produce directions that can survive
implementation without collapsing back into a familiar model-generated style.
A creative packet is a binding explanation of how the design is generated,
not a decorative mood description or a list of fashionable adjectives.

## Shared packet rules

Every direction needs a **content-native generator**: a relationship already
present in the subject that can govern composition, hierarchy, repetition,
imagery, and interaction. Examples include prediction and correction, signal
and interpretation, evidence and conclusion, source and consequence, public
and private, accumulation and release, or another relationship specific to the
actual content. Do not choose a style label as the generator.

Name the model's probable default answer before proposing the deliberate
alternative. Consider composition, typography, palette, imagery, archive or
data structure, navigation, controls, containers, motion, and mobile behavior.
A familiar choice can remain when it is uniquely right for the task, but it
needs a concrete reason.

Make the product job a hard constraint. Originality that hides the primary
content, weakens scanning, or makes the interface unusable is a failed
direction. One memorable risk is useful; novelty is not permission to abandon
hierarchy or legibility.

Use semantic color roles and typography roles rather than naming a palette and
font pair without purpose. Build hierarchy with scale, position, spacing,
width, color, case, and rhythm before assigning heavy font weights everywhere.
Text contact is an intentional exception: avoid accidental overlaps, clipped
glyphs, and near-tangencies, and define how any deliberate layering stays
readable at narrow widths.

When creating a batch, vary the originality method as well as the visual
result. Do not give every concept the same exploration recipe, reference
family, typography archetype, palette logic, or page silhouette.

## Method A: Binding art direction

Use when the user supplies a strong imaginative premise or one emerges clearly
from product context.

1. Define the emotional target and the physical, cultural, or media world that
   gives the direction its logic.
2. Translate that world's principles into composition, type, color, imagery,
   interaction, and mobile behavior.
3. Name the literal motifs and nostalgic or theatrical pastiche to reject.
4. State the primary product job that the premise must not overpower.
5. Challenge whether the premise is visible in the design decisions without an
   explanatory paragraph.

This method should feel specific enough to direct implementation. Do not use it
as a license to make a branded introduction larger than the content users came
to see.

## Method B: Reference collision

Use two or three visually distant references, preferably from outside current
web design: publishing artifacts, performance notation, industrial tools,
packaging, maps, textiles, architecture, scientific material, public signage,
or other subject-relevant fields.

For each reference identify:

- one structural principle to translate;
- one literal motif to reject; and
- why that principle belongs to this product and audience.

Create at least two composition sketches in words. Treat the first plausible
answer as the safest model prior and discard or materially revise it. Compare
the remaining choices for product fit, distinctiveness, legibility, and
transferability to unrelated products. Implement the least transferable
choice that still performs the product's primary job.

## Method C: Anti-convergence

Use when prior generated work exists, the product keeps receiving familiar
designs, or the direction needs a stronger escape from common defaults.

Create a short anti-brief pairing probable defaults with deliberate
alternatives across the important design dimensions. Include the specific
fonts, pairings, palettes, structures, and devices already overused in this
project or batch. Do not rely only on a universal list of AI clichés; identify
the local convergence pattern.

Require:

- a content-native generator;
- a first-viewport silhouette recognizable when blurred;
- one content-bearing visual device;
- hierarchy that changes for a reason instead of random collage;
- a mobile transformation that preserves the generator; and
- a final check that prohibited fallback patterns did not return during
  implementation.

Do not confuse anti-convergence with maximalism. A restrained alternative can
pass when proportion, typography, structure, and content relationships make it
specific.

## Creative packet format

Give each direction a memorable working name and include:

1. **Core idea and product job** — the concept, intended feeling, and the task
   it must perform.
2. **Originality method** — binding art direction, reference collision,
   anti-convergence, or a justified combination.
3. **Content-native generator** — the subject relationship that generates the
   interface.
4. **Inputs and rejections** — reference principles, probable defaults, and
   literal or familiar treatments excluded from this direction.
5. **Composition contract** — first-viewport silhouette, hierarchy, density,
   and page rhythm.
6. **Typography contract** — type roles, sources of contrast, weight
   distribution, reading measures, and collision safeguards.
7. **Color contract** — semantic roles, dominant and scarce colors, and
   contrast constraints.
8. **Imagery or content-bearing visual** — what it communicates and how it is
   treated, including a deliberate no-image solution when relevant.
9. **Interaction and motion** — only behavior that supports meaning,
   orientation, or feedback.
10. **Mobile transformation** — how the generator and signature composition
    survive narrow widths.
11. **Signature moment and aesthetic risk** — what should be remembered and
    what must be tested carefully.
12. **Acceptance and rejection checks** — visible proof that the direction was
    executed, plus the fallback patterns that would invalidate it.

Keep the packet concise enough to guide a builder, but concrete enough that
generic substitutions are recognizable as contract violations.
