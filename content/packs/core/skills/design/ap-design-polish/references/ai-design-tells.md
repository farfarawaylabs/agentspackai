# Generic design signals

Use these as questions about the rendered interface, not a list of forbidden
styles. Familiar patterns can be appropriate, and expressive work can show
restraint. Judge whether each treatment serves this product, audience, and
chosen direction. Never infer AI authorship from appearance.

| Signal worth inspecting | Why it can weaken the result | When it earns its place | Focused correction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Every section or paragraph in a rounded card | Equal framing makes unrelated information look equally important; nested boxes add noise. | Cards represent distinct, actionable objects or useful comparison groups. | Remove redundant inner wrappers or use spacing and headings for secondary grouping. |
| Glass, blur, gradients, glows, or shadows on many surfaces | Competing effects obscure hierarchy, impair readability, and weaken the focal point. | A material treatment is part of the identity or communicates layering and focus. | Keep the treatment where it carries meaning and quiet the supporting surfaces. |
| A giant vague headline and floating dashboard hero | The page communicates a software category without explaining this product. | The actual product view demonstrates the core benefit and the headline makes a specific claim. | Clarify the headline using supported facts, tighten scale, or improve the existing crop. |
| Pills, eyebrows, badges, icons, and accent text all emphasizing the same content | Redundant attention signals dilute emphasis and interrupt reading. | A badge conveys real status or category; an icon aids recognition; an eyebrow locates a section. | Keep the signal with the clearest role and remove duplicate emphasis. |
| Decorative grids, blobs, particles, orbit lines, or abstract shapes | Decoration feels transferable to any product and competes with the content. | It explains the subject or is a coherent signature element of the chosen direction. | Reduce or remove the unrelated layer; preserve the product-specific motif. |
| Arbitrary numbers, metrics, steps, or testimonials as visual filler | Implied order or unsupported proof distracts and can mislead. | Steps express a real sequence; metrics and quotations have verified sources and useful context. | Remove unsupported material and decorative numbering; preserve meaningful sequence and evidence. |
| Many type styles, colors, or alternating section treatments | Every section demands a new reading convention and the page loses rhythm. | Contrast separates real roles or creates deliberate editorial pacing. | Consolidate roles and adjust proportions instead of adding another style. |
| Repeated calls to action or explanatory copy | Interruptions add pressure or repeat what users already understand. | An action is useful after a long section, on mobile, or at a distinct decision point. | Remove interruptions while retaining action access and necessary explanation in each state. |
| Decorative motion or custom controls everywhere | Movement competes with the task; novelty can hide affordances or make controls harder to use. | Motion explains state and continuity; custom interaction provides a real capability. | Quiet secondary motion and simplify treatment while preserving behavior and accessibility. |
| Excessive empty space or compressed detail presented as sophistication | The content becomes harder to scan or read, especially on narrow screens. | Density or whitespace follows the task and the intended composition. | Adjust rhythm locally; do not equate premium design with tiny text or sparse content. |

## Score calibration

The **AI-tell score** describes perceived genericness and overdesign, not the
likelihood of AI authorship. Higher is worse. Use whole numbers and a short
evidence-based explanation; this is a subjective judgment, not a measurement.

- **0–2:** Few supported signals; familiar patterns serve the product and the
  direction feels coherent.
- **3–4:** Some generic or redundant treatments remain, but they do not dominate.
- **5–6:** Repeated defaults or competing emphasis materially weaken identity
  or hierarchy.
- **7–8:** Generic structure and decoration dominate much of the interface.
- **9–10:** Pervasive interchangeable treatment or visual excess overwhelms
  the product's identity and content.

Do not calculate the score by counting patterns. A strongly branded purple
gradient may contribute no weakness; a single dominant generic hero may
matter more than several minor details. Evaluate the full supplied scope and
state which views were inspected. Do not compare scores across different
scope, evidence, or calibration as if they measured a change.

A low score does not establish good usability, accessibility, performance,
conversion, or overall design quality. Verify those outcomes separately.
