# Landing pages

Create a focused argument that helps the right visitor understand the offer,
trust it, and take one appropriate next action. Optimize for qualified outcomes,
not clicks or generic persuasion.

This skill owns audience, message, evidence, page argument, conversion path,
search intent, and measurement. When the task includes visual design or
implementation, also use `ap-frontend-design`. Use `ap-frontend-review` to inspect a
rendered result.

## Confirm that this is a landing page

A landing page serves one primary audience, offer, and action. A homepage may
serve several audiences and navigation intents. Do not force homepage,
documentation, editorial, or product-interface work into a single-offer
landing-page structure.

Identify whether the page is:

- an evergreen indexed page;
- a campaign page reached from ads, email, social, or partners;
- a product-led signup or purchase page;
- a sales-led demo or lead page;
- a waitlist or launch page; or
- a downloadable asset or other lead magnet.

Traffic source and visitor awareness determine what the page must explain. Do
not assume every landing page should rank in search or contain every common
section.

## Build the evidence-backed brief

Read existing product, positioning, customer, analytics, brand, and
`DESIGN.md` material before asking the user for information already present.
Inspect the implementation when revising an existing page.

Establish:

| Decision | Question |
|---|---|
| Audience | Who is this page for right now? |
| Intent | What task or promise brought them here? |
| Outcome | What change do they want? |
| Offer | What exactly will they receive or experience? |
| Mechanism | How does the product create the outcome? |
| Alternative | What do they do today instead? |
| Differentiation | Why is this the best-fit choice? |
| Proof | What can be demonstrated credibly? |
| Objections | Why would a qualified visitor still hesitate? |
| Conversion | What is the lowest-friction useful next step? |
| Measurement | What observable event counts as success? |

Surface important unknowns. Do not fabricate customers, quotations, logos,
ratings, statistics, certifications, integrations, capabilities, pricing, or
results. When proof is missing, mark the gap and recommend the evidence needed.

## Answer the visitor's questions

Make these answers easy to find:

1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What outcome does it create?
4. How does it work?
5. Why should I trust it?
6. What should I do next?
7. Why act now, if there is a truthful reason?

Assume the visitor may be partially informed and skeptical. Move quickly to
fit, differentiation, mechanism, proof, and risk. Do not delay comprehension
with a long brand story or unexplained visual spectacle.

## State the value precisely

Express the outcome, audience, mechanism, and relevant alternative or pain in
plain language. Use this only as a diagnostic, not a mandatory headline
template:

> [Audience] gets [outcome] with [product or category] by [mechanism], without
> [important pain or risk].

Prefer concrete buyer language to category fog such as "revolutionary,"
"next-generation," "AI-powered," or "streamline your workflow." Explain what
the product changes in the visitor's work or life.

For AI products, state what the AI does, what information it uses, where human
control remains, what it does not do, and how privacy, security, reliability,
and failure are handled when relevant.

## Construct an argument, not a template

Choose and order only the sections needed for this audience, traffic source,
awareness level, and buying risk. Possible moves include:

- **Hero:** Identify the offer, audience, outcome, and primary action.
- **Early proof:** Reduce initial skepticism with relevant, verifiable
  evidence.
- **Problem or stakes:** Show the cost of the status quo without exaggerated
  fear.
- **Mechanism or workflow:** Demonstrate how the promise is produced.
- **Benefits or use cases:** Connect capabilities to outcomes for this
  audience.
- **Objections and trust:** Address setup, migration, security, privacy,
  compatibility, pricing, risk, or other real concerns.
- **Customer evidence:** Use named, attributable stories or results.
- **Offer and pricing:** Make commitment, limitations, and terms legible.
- **Comparison or FAQ:** Resolve real late-stage questions with factual answers.
- **Final action:** Restate the relevant value, next step, and what happens
  after conversion.

Do not mechanically produce the familiar hero, logo strip, three cards,
three-step process, testimonial carousel, FAQ, and CTA sequence. The page's
argument and visual composition must arise from the actual offer.

Adapt the evidence and risk reduction to the business:

- **B2B or sales-led:** Emphasize workflow change, integration, security,
  onboarding, migration, and credible business outcomes.
- **Product-led or consumer:** Make the first success immediate, expose trial
  limits, and minimize signup risk.
- **Marketplace:** Demonstrate relevant supply, demand, coverage, and trust on
  both sides.
- **Services:** Use attributable case evidence, process clarity, fit, and
  appropriate qualification.
- **Ecommerce or local:** Make product facts, reviews, delivery, returns,
  availability, location, and hours explicit where relevant.
- **Waitlist or launch:** State what exists today, what is planned, and what
  joining the list actually provides.

## Write for action and trust

- Keep the hero understandable without requiring clever interpretation.
- Use the subhead to add audience, mechanism, scope, or risk reduction.
- Match the page promise and language to the traffic source.
- Use one obvious primary action; secondary actions must support rather than
  compete with it.
- Match the action to readiness: self-serve for low-risk offers, a demo for
  complex evaluation, or the asset itself for a lead magnet.
- Ask forms only for information needed to deliver or prepare the next step.
- Put proof near the claim it supports.
- Resolve the most important objections before the final action.
- Use specific labels that explain what happens next.
- Keep answer-bearing facts clear and standalone while allowing brand voice in
  the surrounding copy.
- Do not use a rotating hero carousel to avoid choosing one primary message.

Use frameworks such as jobs-to-be-done, outcome-first, problem-solution,
before-after-bridge, AIDA, StoryBrand, or objection-led structure only when
they fit the visitor's awareness and decision. Never let a framework erase
product specificity.

## Consider search and answer visibility conditionally

If the page should be indexed or discovered through search or AI-generated
search experiences, read
[references/search-and-citation.md](references/search-and-citation.md).

Use `ap-audit-seo` when the task is to diagnose conventional crawl, indexation,
technical, on-page, or organic-performance issues. Use `ap-audit-geo` when the
task is to audit AI-search eligibility, citations, answer visibility, or AI
referral measurement. Run both only when both discovery channels are actually
in scope.

Do not add search-oriented sections to a private, temporary, or campaign-only
page without a real discovery goal. Do not turn one useful page into many thin
query, industry, or location variants.

## Decide what to test

When performance data exists, diagnose the layer before proposing a fix:

- **Traffic:** The wrong people or promise are reaching the page.
- **Message:** Visitors cannot understand the offer, differentiation, or fit.
- **Trust:** Claims lack evidence or key objections remain unresolved.
- **Friction:** The action, form, pricing, or next step demands too much.
- **Experience:** Layout, speed, accessibility, mobile behavior, or
  interactions impede use.

Test large causal levers before decorative variants:

1. Promise and message match.
2. Offer, action, and CTA wording.
3. Proof type and placement.
4. Form length or step count.
5. Objection order and risk reduction.
6. Pricing or plan framing.
7. Page length, layout, and visual polish.

Define a primary success metric and guardrails. Do not call a variant a winner
without sufficient evidence, and do not optimize raw conversion rate at the
expense of lead quality, activation, retention, refunds, or trust.

## Complete and verify

Before publishing or claiming completion, read
[references/pre-publish-checklist.md](references/pre-publish-checklist.md).

Verify factual claims against current product evidence. Run relevant tests,
builds, structured-data validation, analytics checks, and rendered UI review
when the task includes implementation. State what was not validated.
